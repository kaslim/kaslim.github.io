## Paper facts used by the rebuilt page

- Tasks: cover-song identification (CSI) and music-genre classification (MGC).
- Target systems and datasets: CoverHunter on SHS100K for CSI; IDS-NMR on GTZAN for MGC.
- White-box localization: Grad-CAM on the attacked model's final-block representation.
- Black-box localization: non-overlapping 0.5-second chunks, zero masking with a Tukey window (`alpha = 0.1`), loss-change-per-duration scoring, and coarse-to-fine refinement.
- White-box optimization: reconstruction and untargeted attack losses, grid search over `lambda_rec` and `lambda_att` in `{0.5, 1.0, 2.0}`, with at most 10 iterations.
- Black-box optimization: CMA-ES over the inpainting latent variable, with a 1,000-query budget.
- Evaluation: ASR, post-attack mAP/accuracy, FAD using MERT-V0, LSD, and a 100-participant five-point listening test.
- Table 1 aggregate results:
  - MAIA-WB / CSI: ASR 92.8%, MOS 4.0.
  - MAIA-WB / MGC: ASR 93.5%, MOS 3.8.
  - MAIA-BB / CSI: ASR 80.1%, MOS 3.6.
  - MAIA-BB / MGC: ASR 77.9%, MOS 3.3.

### Paper-internal numerical conflict

The Results prose says MAIA-WB achieves “93.5% for CSI and 94.5% for MGC”, but Table 1 reports 92.8% for CSI and 93.5% for MGC. The rebuilt page uses the formal Table 1 values and does not combine the conflicting prose values.

## Existing page audit

### Traceable paper assets

- `maia/assets/figures/2.png`: paper-derived Grad-CAM figure.
- `maia/assets/figures/3.png`: paper-derived selected-segment figure.

### Random or simulated assets that must not be presented as research output

- `generate_adversarial_samples.py::apply_simple_inpainting` adds Gaussian noise and amplitude modulation; it does not call DiffMusic or GACELA.
- `generate_adversarial_samples.py::generate_mock_importance_heatmap` creates random importance maps.
- `generate_adversarial_samples.py` randomly assigns confidence values and per-sample FAD, LSD, and MOS values.
- `maia/data/samples.json` publishes those random values as predictions, attack success, confidence, and perceptual metrics.
- `maia/js/demo.js` renders those values as successful/failed attacks.
- `maia/data/audio/*_adversarial.wav` was produced by the simplified random-noise pipeline, not by a trained music-inpainting model.

### Untraceable or unsafe-to-republish inputs

- The existing audio filenames and generator source path identify GTZAN-style material, but the repository contains no redistribution permission or per-track provenance. These files are excluded from the new public demo.
- The existing inpainting intervals are hard-coded in the generator. Their centers can be preserved as legacy interface metadata, but they cannot be represented as independently recomputed target-model importance regions.
- The existing `*_importance.json` files are generated by the random heatmap function and are excluded from the new page.

## Publication boundary for the rebuilt demo

Until a target classifier or retrieval model is actually run, the interactive sample can demonstrate only real model-based local regeneration. It must not claim sample-level attack success, prediction changes, confidence, or paper-level metric values. Aggregate Table 1 results are displayed in a separate section.

## Explicit limitations to communicate

- A real inpainting output alone is not a complete MAIA attack reproduction.
- The paper evaluates two MIR tasks and two target-system/dataset pairs.
- White-box MAIA assumes gradients and model access; black-box MAIA assumes query feedback and uses up to 1,000 queries.
- The paper does not establish protection against prompt injection, jailbreaks, tool authorization failures, or trusted-execution failures.
