LSA-Probe / MS-CHN

Was this music used for training?

Access and experimental ground truth

A membership inference attack (MIA) estimates whether a candidate training item belongs to the training set of a specified checkpoint. It provides statistical evidence for data provenance, privacy and model security audits.

The journal training unit is an FMA track. A query uses a fixed crop. Every crop of one track shares its label for that target, even if the exact waveform was never seen. Its label can change with the checkpoint.

Loss baselines and fixed-direction stability use forward queries and intermediate states. Conference LSA-Probe also backpropagates through the reverse computation. MS-CHN additionally requires music known to be absent from the target training set.

Training manifests establish experimental labels and are withheld from the scorer. A positive decision is statistical evidence about the audited training stage. A legal finding requires separate evidence.

Training views and audit queries

All six inspected AudioLDM configurations set mixup to zero.

Training configurations and manifest checks

We train controlled targets with documented training sets so we can check membership against each checkpoint’s manifest. The journal labels concern the denoiser’s training stage. Pretrained components have their own training histories.

Four different changes in the pipeline

  • Preprocessing sets sample rate, channel and amplitude conventions.
  • Training views vary the crop of one track. All six inspected AudioLDM configs set audio mixup to zero. Other augmentation switches lack aligned evidence.
  • Forward Gaussian noise is part of the diffusion process.
  • The audit adds a controlled perturbation δ to an intermediate state.

Reconstruction and stability

The located AudioLDM endpoint baseline predicts a clean latent in one step and measures latent MSE.

Baselines, equations and implementation

Add noise to a candidate and start denoising from the constructed state. Models may recover familiar training data more easily. Musical complexity, timbre, timesteps and noise draws also affect errors.

xt = √ᾱt x0 + √(1 − ᾱt) ε,   ε ∼ N(0, I)

x̂0 = Rθ,t→0(xt)

Erec(x0; t) = d(x̂0, x0)

z0 = Enc(x0)

These equations illustrate waveform diffusion and endpoint error. Latent diffusion applies the noise and reverse steps in z-space. A latent MSE compares internal tensors. A waveform comparison requires decoding.

Diffusion Loss

Compare predicted noise with the injected ε. The located AudioLDM baseline code includes a normalized error norm and MSE variants. Its configuration uses six timestep ratios from 0.10 to 0.80, three seeded draws per ratio and a fixed “music” prompt at guidance scale 1.0.

Endpoint Reconstruction

Compare the recovered endpoint with the clean input. A located journal baseline uses single-step predicted-clean latent MSE. A full reverse rollout defines a different comparison.

SecMI-style posterior error

A music adaptation of step-wise error, comparing a local deterministic forward/return estimate with its reference state.

Other baseline signals

Likelihood Ratio contrasts target and reference denoising evidence. Terminal Denoising Error measures error near the clean end. Embedding NN uses proximity in an embedding space. Lower errors are oriented toward higher membership evidence.

At fixed t with the same noise and ε parameterization, single-step clean-sample MSE equals noise-prediction MSE scaled by (1 − ᾱₜ) / ᾱₜ. They can carry the same evidence.

Probe settings and interpretation

Both branches share the candidate state, conditioning and sampler settings. Paired forward noise or fixed randomness isolates the perturbation in the comparison.

Scale the perturbation by the forward noise amplitude √(1 − ᾱₜ).

Deterministic DDIM, timestep ratio 0.60, ℓ₂ budget up to 0.80, and CDPAM degradation. The paper reports 12 PGD steps, five restarts and ten bisection iterations. The matched-compute comparison uses per-sample wall-clock time on A100-80GB within 5%.

The normalization scale, DDIM randomness, perceptual degradation threshold and final membership cutoff serve distinct purposes. The budget is a search approximation. Member stability is a hypothesis tested on these targets.

Raw Manifold Stability

The historical scorer and the manuscript differ on whether to retain only positive time coordinates.

Probe parameters and aggregation versions

The journal’s Raw Manifold Stability score probes with fixed-amplitude directions using forward computations. Its evidence comes from the response to a small neighborhood of the intermediate state.

At each selected time, divide the paired response distance by the perturbation norm for each seeded direction.

Take the negative high quantile of directional sensitivities. Greater sensitivity gives lower stability.

Transform time coordinates against reference non-members, then aggregate across time.

The draft specifies t/T ∈ {0.55, 0.60, 0.65, 0.70}, η = 0.001, eight directions and direction quantile 0.90. TSS4 names the four time coordinates. H = 2 is the number retained after reference rank transformation and Gaussianization. The inspected AudioLDM wrapper rolls back multiple DDIM steps and returns a latent. The inspected DiffWave pilot uses deterministic reverse steps in waveform space. Historical code sums the top two coordinates even when negative. The final positive-only rule and response implementation still require an aligned artifact.

Raw MS extracts local-response evidence. Similar non-members can still have high raw scores.

MS-CHN

Neighborhood features and score definition

Random cropping gives a training track several possible views. Generalization can give unseen tracks with similar rhythm, harmony, timbre, production or style high stability too. A global comparison can count this shared response as evidence about a particular track. Naturally occurring musical similarity can produce the same problem.

Represent music with frozen CLAP and acoustic descriptors. Fit preprocessing on known reference non-members in C_ref.

Retrieve k nearest reference non-members. Similarity selects the comparison group.

Estimate the upper quantile of their Raw MS scores and subtract it from the candidate score. The draft uses k = 200 and q = 0.95.

Qlocalref(x) = Quantileq{SMS(u) ∶ u ∈ Nkref(x)}

SCHNref(x) = SMS(x) − Qlocalref(x)

A located feature export has 512 CLAP and 93 acoustic columns (605 total). An earlier export reported 87 acoustic and 599 joint. The method draft describes L₂-normalized CLAP, reference-fitted robust acoustic scaling and concatenation. Historical code uses Euclidean neighbors. Final column selection, scaling and family weights await the aligned configuration.

A positive residual places a candidate above its local quantile. The final decision uses a separate 1% calibration. MS-CHN addresses similarity-related score variation. The role of augmentation remains a mechanism hypothesis.

The score estimates excess stability relative to similar non-members. It cannot identify which crop was presented during training or reconstruct an unaugmented original.

Reference, calibration and evaluation

Protocol details, sources and citation

Fit the full scoring map before threshold calibration. In each target and fold, check role disjointness and ground truth using original track IDs.

Known non-members fit per-time score transforms and feature preprocessing, and populate the local score library. Exclude the query’s own track when scoring reference items.

A separate set of known non-members calibrates the decision after the score mapping is frozen.

Held-out non-members measure the empirical false-positive rate.

Members held out of score fitting measure the true-positive rate, or member recall.

p(x) = [1 + #{u ∈ Cthr ∶ S(u) ≥ S(x)}] / (|Cthr| + 1)

For the rank-calibrated rule, flag when p(x) ≤ 0.01. This rank measures score extremeness among calibration non-members. It does not estimate the probability that a query is a member. Empirical FPR can vary across finite batches and subgroups.

Draft design and aggregation

The September design summary specifies five rotating non-member folds, with 5,000 reference tracks, 2,000 threshold-calibration tracks, 1,000 evaluation non-members and 500 evaluation members per target and fold. Choose configurations on M1/M3/M5 and D1/D3/D5; then evaluate on M2/M4/M6 and D2/D4/D6. Aggregate within target first and use independently trained targets as the units of cross-target variation.

Role manifests and aligned scores are needed to verify those sizes, disjointness, leave-one-out and near-duplicate rules. Earlier manuscript tables describe a shared calibration pool. Repeated tracks across folds do not create new independent observations.

What the journal experiments ask

  1. Do false positives concentrate? The earlier draft reports a 4.64× average top-bin lift for Raw MS in music-matched neighborhoods and 1.21× with random neighborhoods. Lift is top-bin FPR divided by overall FPR. Values above 1 indicate concentration.
  2. Does probing help? The draft reports AUC 0.6267 and 9.19% recall for Raw MS. These describe signal on its evaluated targets and operating point.
  3. Does matching help? The draft-reported joint feature row increases recall. The ablation and historical score replay differ in their label and calibration provenance, so the size of the gain awaits reconciliation.

Similarity-conditioned scoring can leave uneven false-positive risk. An augmentation-on/off effect needs matched training runs. Results are specific to these datasets, model families and access assumptions.
