LSA-Probe

Membership Inference Attack Against Music Diffusion Models via Generative Manifold Perturbation

We replace a noisy endpoint signal with a behavioral stability score: the minimum time-normalized latent perturbation required to cross a fixed perceptual degradation threshold.

01

Audit question

What are we auditing?

Given a candidate audio clip and an audited music diffusion model, estimate whether the clip was used during training.

01Candidate audio clip
02Audited music diffusion model
03Was this clip used during training?

Member

The clip belongs to the target model’s training set.

Non-member

The clip was not included in the target model’s training set.

Evidence boundary

Membership inference produces a statistical audit signal. It is not training-data extraction, model inversion, attribute inference, or standalone legal proof of copyright infringement.

02

Access assumptions

A strong, explicit white-box threat model

LSA-Probe is designed for a developer or authorised auditor who can inspect and differentiate through the model’s reverse process.

Actor Developer / authorised auditor
Access Parameters, sampler, intermediate states, gradients
Output Statistical membership audit score
  • Can run forward diffusion and deterministic reverse sampling.
  • Can compute gradients with respect to an intermediate diffusion state.
  • Does not need shadow models or member labels from the target model.
  • Does need an independent non-member development set for calibration.
Operational boundary

LSA-Probe is best understood as an authorised internal audit method, not a remote black-box attack available to an ordinary user.

03

Why the baseline is weak

Why endpoint reconstruction error is insufficient

A single reconstruction loss is a noisy endpoint observation, especially in the strict low-FPR regime that matters for high-cost audits.

Timestep

Reconstruction error changes with the diffusion state.

Randomness

Injected noise and sampling choices alter the endpoint.

Content

Timbre and musical complexity can confound raw loss.

Perception

Waveform MSE need not track what listeners perceive.

Instead of asking whether one reconstruction has a small error, LSA-Probe asks how much worst-case, time-normalized intervention is required to destabilise the local reverse process.

04

Latent stability hypothesis

Measure the intervention required to break local stability

Training members empirically occupy more stable local regions of the learned reverse mapping. They require a larger bounded perturbation to reach the same perceptual degradation target.

Audit score

Cadv(x) = min η

Minimum perturbation budget that reaches the fixed perceptual degradation target.

Cadv(member) > Cadv(non-member)

This is an empirical local-geometric interpretation, not an analytic recovery of a data manifold or a universal claim about diffusion models.

A critical distinction

Two thresholds, two different jobs

1

Perceptual degradation threshold τ

D(x̂0, x̂0δ) ≥ τ

Defines the degradation target. It is calibrated as P95 on an independent non-member development set at ηref=0.05. It is not the membership cutoff.

2

Adversarial cost Cadv

Measures the minimum time-normalized budget required to reach τ for each candidate.

3

Membership decision cutoff

A separate cutoff on Cadv defines the operating point, such as FPR=1%, and yields TPR@1% FPR.

05

Two-loop adversarial probe

Inner PGD finds direction; outer bisection finds cost

The method searches for a strong local degradation direction at each budget, then finds the smallest successful budget.

  1. 01

    Forward diffuse the candidate to an intermediate state.

  2. 02

    Inject a time-normalized bounded perturbation.

  3. 03

    Use inner PGD to find the strongest degradation direction.

  4. 04

    Use outer bisection to find the minimum successful budget.

δt = σt δ̃    σt = √(1−ᾱt)

The raw scale of an intervention is not comparable across diffusion timesteps because the forward noise variance changes with t. Scaling δ̃ by σt places the perturbation on the timestep’s natural noise scale.

What the result does—and does not—show

LSA-Probe consistently improves the strict low-FPR operating point, but the best TPR at 1% FPR is 20%. The method therefore provides a stronger audit signal without solving membership auditing outright. Precision still depends on the real member base rate.

07

Reliability and limitations

How the evidence was controlled

The evaluation spans waveform and latent diffusion, two music domains, leakage controls, uncertainty estimates and compute-matched baselines.

Models

DiffWave · waveform DDPM
MusicLDM · VAE latent diffusion

Datasets

MAESTRO v3 · solo piano
FMA-Large · multi-genre music

Main setup

Deterministic DDIM · t_ratio=0.6 · p=2 · ηmax=0.8 · CDPAM · 22.05 kHz training audio · in-graph resampling to 16 kHz for metrics.

Split controls

Piece/artist splits for MAESTRO; track/artist splits for FMA; Chromaprint + LSH duplicate and cover checks with manual review.

Statistical protocol

DeLong AUC intervals; sample-level bootstrap elsewhere; 10,000 resamples; Holm–Bonferroni correction.

Compute accounting

B=10 outer × K=12 inner, roughly (K+2)B reverse passes per sample; baselines matched within ±5% wall-clock on an A100-80GB.

Scientific boundary

Limitations and open questions

  1. Strong white-box access is required: parameters, sampler, intermediate states and gradients.
  2. Repeated PGD, restarts and bisection make the audit computationally expensive.
  3. The best TPR@1% FPR is still only 20%.
  4. Evaluation covers two model families and two music datasets.
  5. Threshold calibration depends on an independent non-member development set.
  6. Cross-model and cross-domain threshold transfer remain underexplored.
  7. Results do not directly generalise to text LLMs or multimodal agents.
  8. A membership score is not standalone legal evidence.
  9. “Generative manifold” is an empirical local-geometric description, not an analytically recovered manifold.
  10. Defences need further study: differential privacy, regularisation, deduplication, audit-aware training and reduced memorisation.

08

Future direction

From Generative-Model Auditing to Agent Safety

A methodological transfer, not an experimental result of LSA-Probe.

LSA-Probe
Define the protected training-data asset

Agent Safety
Define protected user data, memory, retrieved content and tool permissions

LSA-Probe
Specify white-box developer-side access

Agent Safety
Build authorised internal red-team and audit infrastructure

LSA-Probe
Measure the minimum intervention needed to destabilise a trajectory

Agent Safety
Measure the minimum bounded intervention needed to change a decision or tool action

LSA-Probe
Evaluate at strict low-FPR operating points

Agent Safety
Control false alarms before blocking legitimate tasks or users

LSA-Probe
Treat membership as statistical evidence

Agent Safety
Preserve audit trails and uncertainty rather than issuing absolute claims

What transfers

Asset definition, authorised access, bounded intervention, strict operating points and uncertainty-aware audit trails.

What does not transfer directly

Audio latents are not LLM hidden states; diffusion trajectories are not agent plans. LSA-Probe does not detect prompt injection, stop jailbreaks, solve tool authorisation, provide trusted execution or establish effectiveness for mobile agents.

Possible research direction

Generalise stability-based auditing from generative trajectories to agent decision trajectories: measure the minimum bounded intervention required to trigger an unsafe decision while jointly tracking task utility and side effects.
